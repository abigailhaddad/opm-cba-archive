II.       DISCUSSION

          A.       Enforcing the PI Order

          The Court first addresses Plaintiffs’ request that this Court enforce the PI Order.

“Ensuring compliance with a prior order is an equitable goal which a court is empowered to

pursue even absent a finding of contempt.” Berger v. Heckler, 771 F.2d 1556, 1569 (2d Cir.

1985). “In enforcing its orders, a district court may take such steps as are appropriate given the

resistance of the noncompliant party.” Id.; In re Tronox Inc., 855 F.3d 84, 112 (2d Cir. 2017)

(noting that a district “court’s choice of how to enforce the order is reviewed for abuse of

discretion” and that “a court can take any reasonable action to secure compliance” (cleaned

up)); see also United States v. Visa U.S.A., Inc., No. 98-CV-7076, 2007 WL 1741885, at *12

(S.D.N.Y. June 15, 2007) (enforcing a court order “to give present effect” to the judgment, not

as a sanction).

          There are two distinct issues before the Court on Plaintiffs’ motion for enforcement.

First, whether the re-termination of the CBA constituted a violation of the PI Order. Second,

               1.     Re-termination

       Regarding the October 7, 2026 re-termination, the core issue before the Court is

whether Defendants were required to move the Court to either dissolve, amend, or clarify the

PI Order before unilaterally re-terminating the CBA. Plaintiffs argue that Defendants

improperly engaged in “self help” by terminating the CBA without first applying to the Court.

Defendants, in response, contend that they were not required to move the Court for dissolution

of the PI Order because the re-termination constituted a new agency action. The Court agrees

with Plaintiffs.

       Even when party disagrees with a court’s imposition of injunctive relief, “persons

subject to an injunctive order issued by a court with jurisdiction are expected to obey that

decree until it is modified or reversed, even if they have proper grounds to object to the order.”

JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. v. VTB Bank, P.J.S.C., No. 24-CV-2924, 2024 WL 3567418, at

*5 (S.D.N.Y. July 29, 2024), aff’d, No. 24-2327, 2025 WL 1983432 (2d Cir. July 17, 2025)

(cleaned up). “It is a matter ‘of respect for the judicial process.’” Paramedics Electromedicina

Comercial, Ltda v. GE Med. Sys. Info. Techs., Inc., 369 F.3d 645, 656 (2d Cir. 2004) (quoting

GTE Sylvania, Inc. v. Consumers Union, 445 U.S. 375, 387 (1980)). “Disagreements with


                                                8
         Case 3:25-cv-01907-VDO        Document 84        Filed 10/16/26      Page 9 of 14




judicial decisions must be resolved through motions, stays, and appeals, not through unilateral

noncompliance.” Am. Fed’n of Gov’t Emp., AFL-CIO v. Noem, No. 25-CV-451, 2026 WL

113599, at *3 (W.D. Wash. Jan. 15, 2026) (cleaned up); see also Maness v. Meyers, 419 U.S.

449, 458 (1975) (“If a person to whom a court directs an order believes that order is incorrect

the remedy is to appeal, but, absent a stay, he must comply promptly with the order pending

appeal.”).

         Relying on Biden v. Texas, 597 U.S. 785 (2022), Defendants assert the Court is barred

from reviewing the re-termination because it is a new agency action, and the Court may only

adjudicate the specific agency action Plaintiffs put at issue (here, the September 25, 2025,

termination).34 Defendants failed to mention, however, that in Biden v. Texas, the Government

agency continued complying with the District Court’s injunction, despite issuing a new action,

and then properly moved to vacate the injunction. 597 U.S. at 796. Defendants in that case

stated that the new agency action “would be implemented as soon as practicable after a final

judicial decision to vacate.” Id. (cleaned up). At no point did the defendants in Biden v. Texas

seek to disregard a court order. Instead, they complied with the injunction, despite issuing a

new action, and only sought to implement it after moving to vacate the original injunction.

Biden v. Texas is thus distinguishable, as it does not bear on a district court’s equitable remedial

powers to secure compliance with an injunction.

         Here, in contrast, the Defendants failed to move the Court for amendment, clarification,

or dissolution. Instead, it filed a “Notice of Compliance,” and construed Plaintiffs’ (and the

Court’s) lack of an immediate response to the Notice as an implicit grant of authority to re-

terminate the CBA. But a “Notice of Compliance” is an inferior substitute for appropriately

moving the Court to amend, clarify, or dissolve the PI Order. Only a week before re-

termination, the Court ordered Defendants to “immediately reinstate the CBA for the

remainder of the agreed-upon term provided in the CBA.”35 The agreed upon term of

expiration for the CBA is May 28, 2029.36 Rather than abiding by this order, Defendants issued

the Re-termination Memorandum, which purported to terminate the CBA under new rationale.

But an agency may not use “new rationale” to justify a “unilateral decision to abandon its

obligations under the injunction.” Am. Fed’n of Gov’t Emp., 2026 WL 113599, at *3.

         Accordingly, in exercising its jurisdiction to interpret and enforce its own prior order,

In re Tronox Inc., 855 F.3d at 112, the Court grants Plaintiffs’ motion to enforce the PI Order

as it pertains to the October 7, 2026, re-termination of the CBA. The re-termination is VOID

because Defendants unilaterally terminated the CBA without first moving the Court to modify

the PI Order. The CBA shall remain in effect until either the expiration of the agreed upon

term of the agreement on May 28, 2029, or until the Court grants a properly filed motion by

Defendants to dissolve or clarify the PI Order to permit re-termination.

under the agreement itself. The CBA has an extensive grievance process for managing

individual complaints of noncompliance. Those concerns are properly addressed under the

CBA—not by this Court. Accordingly, the Court will not exercise jurisdiction over every

compliance dispute when the agreement provides a grievance process to handle those exact

issues.

         B.     Civil Contempt

         The Court also denies Plaintiffs’ request to hold Defendants in civil contempt for failing

to reinstate or for re-terminating the CBA.42 While the Court has the authority to issue a civil

contempt finding “for failure to comply with a Court order,” Paramedics Electromedicina, 369

F.3d at 655, doing so at this stage is premature. Because the Court has already issued an order

enforcing the preliminary injunction and voiding the October 7, 2026, re-termination of the

CBA, a finding of civil contempt is not necessary at this time.

the PI Order before unilaterally re-terminating the CBA. The CBA shall remain in effect until

either the expiration of the agreement on May 28, 2029, or until the Court grants a properly

filed motion by Defendants to dissolve or clarify the PI Order to permit re-termination.43 In

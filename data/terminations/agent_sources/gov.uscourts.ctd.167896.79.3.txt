SUBJECT:                     Termination of Existing Collective Bargaining Agreement

This memorandum provides official notice to the American Federation of Government
Employees (AFGE) and the Council of Prisons Locals-33 (CPL or CPL-33) that, pursuant to
Executive Order (EO) 14251 , the Federal Bureau of Prisons (Bureau or BOP) is terminating the
Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA or Master Agreement) between the BOP and CPL-33
(effective July 21, 2014-May 28, 2029), as well as any amendments, local supplemental
agreements, past practices, and memoranda of understanding at all levels for all employees
covered by EO 14251 , effective the date of this memorandum.

                 I.      Background

Pursuant to the Master Agreement, CPL-33, which is a subsidiary of AFGE, is the sole and
exclusive representative of covered Bureau employees. That relationship led to the issuance of
the Master Agreement between CPL-33 and the BOP. The most recent version of the Master
Agreement was issued on July 21 , 2014, and extended (with amendments) up to and through
May 28, 2029.

On March 27, 2025, President Donald J. Trump signed EO 14251 , "Exclusions from Federal
Labor-Management Relations Programs," which amended EO 12171, "Exclusions from the
Federal Labor-Management Relations Program," issued November 19, 1979, which sets forth
the Federal agencies and subdivisions thereof excluded from coverage under the Federal Service
Labor-Management Relations Statute (FSLMRS), 5 U.S.C. Chapter 71.

Section 2 of the Executive Order excludes DOJ, except for particular offices of the United States
Marshals Service, from coverage under the FSLMRS. BOP is among those components
affirmatively excluded from coverage by the Executive Order. Although Section 2 of the
Executive Order includes an exception for police officers, security guards, and firefighters, this
exception explicitly does not apply to employees of the BOP.

In issuing EO 14251 , President Trump determined, consistent with the authority granted to him
under 5 U.S.C. § 7103(b)(l), that: (1) the BOP has a primary function of intelligence,
counterintelligence, investigative, or national security work; and (2) the provisions of the
FSLMRS cannot be applied to the BOP in a manner consistent with national security

On April 25, 2025, the Office of Personnel Management (OPM) issued guidance advising

Counsels to assess next steps regarding those CBAs."

Following the issuance of the revised guidance, and pursuant to EO 1425 1, on September 25,
2025, the BOP terminated the CBA with CPL-33 along with any other negotiated agreements or
MOUs between the BOP, CPL-33, and any local chapters. On February 12, 2026, OPM again
advised agencies covered by EO 14251 that they should "tenninate or modify all CBAs" for
units impacted by EO 14251.

On September 29, 2026, pursuant to a court order issued by the United States District Court,
District of Connecticut (3:25-CV-1907, Dkt. No. 72), the BOP's CBA with CPL-33 was
reinstated . On October 2, 2026, the Agency notified the Union that it was aware of the Court's
September 29, 2026, Order, and was complying with its tenn s.

The Court's Order was based solely on the conclusion that Bureau leadership had issued
inconsistent rationales for terminating the CBA; that order does not preclude the Bureau from
issuing a new tennination decision after reconsidering the basis for that action. See 3 :25-CV-
O1907, Dkt. No. 74. The Agency filed a Notice of Compliance with the Court, id., noting this
interpretation. Neither the Court nor the Union has indicated any disagreement regarding the
Agency's ability to re-terminate the CBA in a new decision. As such, on October 7, 2026, the
Bureau rescinded the previous termination of the CBA, dated September 25, 2025. The Bureau
now hereby re-terminates the CBA, based on the following rationales.

           II.     Termination of the BOP-AFGE/CPL-33 CBA

As stated above, this memorandum provides notice that effective immediately, BOP is
terminating the Master Agreement, as well as any and all amendments, local supplemental
agreements, and memoranda of understanding that were in effect subsidiary to the Master
Agreement, for all covered BOP employees. The independent bases for this termination decision
are explained below:

               1. The President Excluded the BOP from the FSLMRS' Coverage and the CBA
                  Was Not Intended to Operate Independent of the FSLRMS

The President, acting pursuant to 5 U.S.C. § 7 103(b)(1 ), validly exercised his authority in
Executive Order 14251 to exclude the BOP from coverage under the FSLMRS. Because multiple
provisions central to the CBA derive their force and operation solely from the statutory
framework established by the FSLMRS, it is clear that the CBA was not intended to function
independently of that statute.

Under the FSLMRS, employees and unions possess statutory rights that exist only under that
chapter. This means that the rights afforded to employees and unions under the FSLMRS- such
as an employee's "right ... to act for a labor organization in the capacity of a representative" or
"to engage in collective bargaining with respect to conditions of employment through
representatives chosen by employees under this chapter," id. § 7102; or a union's right to be an
exclusive representative, id. § 7111 , or "to act for, and negotiate collective bargaining agreements
covering, all employees in the unit," id. § 71 14-are no longer in effect at the BOP.

The CBA itself presupposes the continuing applicability of the FSLMRS. Several key provisions,
including those governing official time, 1 grievance procedures, 2 and arbitration,3 depend entirely
on statutory authority that no longer applies to BOP. Without the FSLMRS, these provisions lack
both legal foundation and enforceability. For example, the CBA anticipates review of arbitral
awards before the FLRA and, in some circumstances, a right to judicial review of FLRA orders
on such awards,4 but the authority for FLRA review of arbitral decisions stems from the
FSLMRS, which is no longer applicable to the BOP.

Because the FSLMRS no longer applies, the Bureau has no ongoing statutory obligation to
bargain, to recognize AFGE or CPL-33 as exclusive representatives, or to remain bound by
CB As previously negotiated under the FSLMRS. In light of the exclusion of BOP from the
FSLMRS framework, BOP has reconsidered the appropriateness of maintaining a CBA with
CPL-33. Maintaining the CBA despite the President's exclusion order has already caused, and
will continue to cause, significant and irreparable harm to the Bureau. These hanns include
substantial monetary, operational, and resource impacts relating to staffing and scheduling,
implementation of the First Step Act, staff and inmate safety, necessary infrastructure repairs,
adoption of technological advancements, and updating mission-critical policies and procedures.
As discussed in Section 2, these monetary, operational, and resource impacts directly interfere
with the Agency's ability to meet its national-security duties and responsibilities, as well as its
other duties and responsibilities.

    The FSLMRS reflects Congress's decision that, at most agencies, the benefits of collective
    bargaining outweigh the inefficiencies that such bargaining creates. But Congress made a
    different policy decision with regard to agencies and subdivisions of agencies designated under 5
    U.S.C. § 7103(b)(1 ). With regard to those agencies designated because of their national security
    functions, Congress concluded that the inefficiencies created by granting employees collective-
    bargaining rights would not-or at least may not-outweigh the benefits of such bargaining.
    The BOP agrees with this conclusion, and in light of the inefficiencies that bargaining with CPL-
    33 has created, the BOP has decided to terminated the CBA.

    Termination of this CBA is therefore an appropriate consequence of Executive Order 1425 1. In
    light of the above costs and to fully and faithfully implement the President's directive, the
    Bureau has concluded that termination at the earliest practicable date is appropriate.

                   2. Termination of the CBA Is Necessary for the BOP to Fully Implement Its
                      National Security Functions Pursuant to EO 14251

The President issued EO 1425 1, making a national security determination regarding the BOP,
among other agencies, and concluding that the FSLMRS could not be applied to the BOP in a
manner consistent with national security requirements and considerations. Notably, the BOP has
direct and primary national security functions, including: (1) incarcerating individuals, including
domestic and foreign terrorists, who threaten the safety of Americans, American institutions, and
the sovereignty of the United States; (2) monitoring inmate communications to detect and disrupt
public safety threats, criminal activities, and terrorist organizations operating within the United
States and internationally; and (3) serving as members of both the National Joint Terrorism
Taskforce and the FBI Joint Terrorism Task Force. These task forces serve as an integrated force
of federal, state, and local law enforcement agencies which combat terrorism on a national and
international scale.

As a member of the larger Department of Justice (DOJ) law enforcement community, BOP also
works to improve the overall readiness of the United States to respond to various domestic and
international threats, including natural disasters, terroristic threats, and other conflicts. For
example, the BOP has been instrumental in assisting the Department of Homeland Security in its
effort to arrest, detain, house, and deport individuals who enter the United States without
appropriate authorization. The BOP also actively supports national, state, and local emergency
management efforts by providing resources and personnel as needed.

The EO reflects both the important national security role BOP performs and how collective
bargaining negatively affects the BOP's readiness and ability to perform that mission. In addition
to the President's constitutional responsibilities for protecting national security, Congress
statutorily tasked the President with determining when collective bargaining and other labor
rights afforded under the FSLMRS are incompatible with national security considerations.5 The
BOP accordingly credits the President's determination and affirmative exclusion of BOP, as it
must, and concludes the continued application of the FSLMRS is inconsistent with the Bureau's
national-security duties and responsibilities.

    After considering the above, the BOP has determined that continued adherence to the CBA is

    requirements and considerations, as determined by the President, in EO 14251.

                III.    Agency Termination Notice

Effective the date of this memorandum and pursuant to EO 14251 , Exclusions from Federal
Labor-Management Relations Programs, the CBA between the BOP and AFGE/CPL-33 (July
21, 2014-May 28, 2029), as well as any and all amendments, local supplemental agreements, and
memoranda of understanding that were in effect subsidiary to the Master Agreement, are
terminated and no longer in effect. As a result, based on the EO and as of the date of this notice,
the Bureau of Prisons no longer recognizes the Counsel of Prison Locals, AFGE, as the exclusive
representative for employees of the existing bargaining unit.

To the extent that any employee developed reliance interests on the CBA, the BOP has
considered such interests in deciding to terminate the CBA now and does not consider them to
outweigh the importance of fully implementing the EO and ensuring consistency with the
President's national-security-related conclusion regarding collective-bargaining activities at the
BOP. Importantly, following the termination of the CBA, employees will remain fully covered
by, among other authorities, applicable provisions of Title 5, United States Code, and regulations
issued by OPM; merit system principles and prohibited personnel practice protections; equal
employment opportunity and anti-discrimination laws; workplace safety requirements; and the
BOP's internal policies and directives (other than those required by or found in its CBA),
including BOP 's internal grievance procedure. 6 While these authorities may differ from the rights
and obligations contained in the CBA, they provide fair and enforceable standards governing
pay, leave, hours of work, perfonnance management, discipline, adverse actions, workplace
safety, and employee protections that, unlike the CBA's provisions, may be applied consistently
with the BOP's national security functions, requirements, and considerations.

To the extent that a union has developed reliance interests on the CBA, those interests are
necessarily tempered by the possibility that the President will, as he has here and as other
Presidents have done with respect to various agencies dating back to President Carter, exercise
their authority under the FSLMRS to remove an agency from that statute's coverage. In any
event, any such interests do not outweigh the important goals the BOP seeks to accomplish by
tenninating this CBA.

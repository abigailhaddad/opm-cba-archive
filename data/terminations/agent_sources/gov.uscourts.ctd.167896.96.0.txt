                          DEFENDANTS’ OPPOSITION TO
                 PLAINTIFFS’ MOTION FOR LEAVE TO SUPPLEMENT

       Pursuant to Local Civil Rule 7 and the Court’s Order, ECF No. 89, Defendants, the Federal

Bureau of Prisons (“BOP”) and William K. Marshall III, in his official capacity as Director of the

BOP, hereby respectfully respond in opposition to Plaintiffs’ motion for leave to file a

supplemental complaint pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 15(d), ECF No. 91 (“Motion

to Supplement”). In the Motion to Supplement, Plaintiffs state they “seek to supplement solely to

add facts and related claims based on events that have occurred since Plaintiffs filed their initial

complaint on November 13, 2025. Of particular note, following this Court’s order granting

Plaintiffs’ motion for preliminary injunction, BOP has made the final decision to re-terminate the

[Collective Bargaining Agreement (“CBA”)]. On October 7, 2026, the Bureau of Prisons released

a memorandum purporting to effectuate that re-termination.” Id., p. 2 (citing ECF No. 79 at 1, ECF

No. 79-1). As the Court voided Defendants’ attempted re-termination, it would be futile to permit

supplementation on this basis, as such supplementation could not “address the legal consequences

of that subsequent conduct in this action” because the action has already been voided. Id., p. 3.

   I.      BACKGROUND

        On September 29, 2026, the Court granted Plaintiff’s Preliminary Injunction (“PI”) motion.

See PI Order, ECF No. 72. In the PI Order, the Court found the BOP Director’s termination letter

and public message were contradictory and, “[a]lthough an agency may have multiple reasons for

its action, it cannot offer an entirely pretextual explanation that is undermined by its own

contradictory assertions made the same day.” Id., p. 26. As such, the Court concluded that

“Plaintiffs are likely to succeed on their claim that Defendants’ decision to terminate the CBA was

arbitrary and capricious in violation of the APA.” Id., p. 27. The Court also found Plaintiffs

demonstrated that they will suffer irreparable harm that could not otherwise be remedied in the

absence of a preliminary injunction and that the balance of the equities and the public interest also

weigh in favor of granting a preliminary injunction. Id., pp. 27-30. In granting the preliminary

injunction, the Court concluded with an order that “Defendants shall immediately reinstate the

CBA, as well as any amendments, local supplemental agreements, and memoranda of

understanding that were in place subsidiary to the CBA, for the remainder of the agreed-upon term

provided in the CBA.” Id., p. 32. Earlier in its PI Order, the Court also stated that “Defendants

shall immediately reinstate the CBA for the remainder of the agreed-upon term provided in the

CBA, and shall be governed by the terms of that agreement.” Id., p. 2.

        On October 2, 2026, Defendants notified the Court of their intention to comply with the PI

Order by filing a Notice of Compliance. See Notice, ECF No. 74. The Defendants made clear in

the Notice “that they did not understand the Court’s order to: (1) preclude the BOP from re-

terminating the CBA in a fresh decision before the end of the CBA’s term; or (2) mean that the

Court intends to supervise every instance in which the BOP is alleged to have violated the CBA.”

Id.; see also Enforcement Order, pp. 2-3. Plaintiffs did not respond directly to the Notice of

   II.      LEGAL STANDARD

         The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure provide that, “[o]n motion and reasonable notice, the

court may, on just terms, permit a party to serve a supplemental pleading setting out any

transaction, occurrence, or event that happened after the date of the pleading to be supplemented.”

Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(d). The Court has discretion to grant a Rule 15(d) motion “upon reasonable

notice and upon such terms as may be just.” Quaratino v. Tiffany & Co., 71 F.3d 58, 66 (2d Cir.

1995) (“[L]eave ... should be freely permitted when the supplemental facts connect it to the original

pleading.”). “Absent undue delay, bad faith, dilatory tactics, undue prejudice to the party to be

served with the proposed pleading, or futility, the motion should be freely granted.” Id. (citing

   III.      ARGUMENT

          As the Court voided Defendants attempted re-termination of the CBA, it would be futile to

permit supplementation on this basis, as such supplementation could not address the legal

consequences of a since-voided action.

          “Courts in the Second Circuit apply the same standards to motions to supplement a

complaint under Rule 15(d) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure as to motions to amend under

Rule 15(a)(2).” Sabir, 2022 WL 1291731, at *11 (citing Shubert v. Town of Glastonbury, No.

3:18cv00112 (MPS), 2020 WL 6395472, at *1 (D. Conn. Nov. 2, 2020). While supplementation

or amendment may be appropriate “[i]f the underlying facts or circumstances relied upon by a

plaintiff may be a proper subject of relief,” Foman 371 U.S. at 182, it follows that supplementation

would not be appropriate if the underlying facts or circumstances are not a proper subject of relief.

Indeed “[a] claim may be futile ‘if the proposed change advances a claim or defense that is legally

insufficient on its face,’ in which case ‘the court may deny leave to amend.’” Betts v. Armstrong,

No. 1:25cv341, 2025 WL 1435105, at *1 (M.D.N.C. May 19, 2025), aff’d, No. 25-1607, 2025 WL

2438193 (4th Cir. Aug. 25, 2025) (quoting Williams v. Little Rock Mun. Water Works, 21 F.3d

218, 225 (8th Cir. 1994)).

          In the Motion to Supplement, Plaintiffs state that “[i]n opposing this motion, Defendants

have taken the position that Plaintiffs should instead be required to file an amended complaint

rather than a supplemental complaint.” Motion to Supplement, p. 3, ECF No. 91. Defendants stated

their position to Plaintiffs prior to the Court voiding BOP’s re-termination of the CBA. In the

prayer for relief contained within the proposed supplemental complaint, Plaintiffs ask the court to

the First Amendment.” Id., p. 13. As it stands now, neither supplementation or amendment would

new claims set forth in their proposed supplemental complaint. As such, it would be futile to permit

supplementation on this basis.

         As the Court voided Defendants’ attempted re-termination of the CBA, it would be futile

to permit supplementation on this basis, as such supplementation could not address the legal

consequences of a since-voided action. Defendants respectfully request this Court deny Plaintiffs’

Motion to Supplement as futile.

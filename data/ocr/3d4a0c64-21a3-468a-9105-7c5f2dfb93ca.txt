ARTICLE 1 DEFINITIONS

SECTION 1. UNION-MANAGEMENT MEETINGS: Meetings which are held for
communication and exchange of views with the intent of agreeing on matters of mutual
interest.

SECTION 2. DAYS: Calendar days unless otherwise specified. In computing time limits,
the day following the day the action is taken is considered the first day.

SECTION 3. UNION OFFICIAL AND/OR UNION REPRESENTATIVE: Any duly
authorized representative of the AFGE Local 2185 (the Union) and elected officials or
appointed officials of the union including stewards. and alternates, in accordance with
the provisions of the agreement.

SECTION 4. SENIORITY: Seniority in the organization (lowest in branch or division, not
shift or squad) will be: determined using the following factors, in order: First: Date in
which the employee joins the organization; Second: Time employed at Dugway Proving
Ground (in any civilian federal service organization); Third: Service Computation Date
(Total Federal Service); Fourth: Service Computation Date (Leave) Seniority. Fifth,
order of arrival on the first-day in current position:

SECTION 5. IN-GRADE SENIORITY: The most recent date an employee was
permanently hired to a new position inside of their respective organization. In case of a
tie, seniority will be determined using the factors listed in Section 4..

SECTION 6. OPEN SEASON:

a. Regular Days Off (RDO): A period from 1 January through 31 January in which
Bargaining Unit Employees (BUEs) will be allowed to project and submit a bid for
RDO and Shift Assignments (SA).

b. Shift assignments will be completed and published by 15 February. The effective
date each year of the shift assignments will be the start of the first full pay period
in April.

c. Leave: From 15 February to 15 March employees will be allowed to project and

request leave for the leave year. Employees will be notified no later than 31 March
of the status of their leave request submitted (approved ‘or disapproved).

SECTION 7. PARTIES: Refers to both AFGE 2185 and Dugway Proving Ground
(Management) (As defined by Bargaining Unit (BU) Certification].

SECTION 8. MID-TERM BARGAINING: is that negotiation which takes place between
the parties at the midpoint of the term of the agreement if requested by either party
within 30 days of the. midpoint.2019

ARTICLE 3 GOVERNING LAWS AND REGULATIONS

SECTION 1: In the administration of all matters covered by this agreement, the parties
are governed by applicable laws, government wide regulations, and agency regulations
that were in effect-at the time the agreement was negotiated, as well as agency
regulations implemented after the execution of this agreement that have been properly
negotiated between the parties to the extent required by law.

SECTION 2: Where an agency regulation implemented after the execution of this
agreement conflicts with this agreement, the provisions of this agreement shall control,
absent mutual agreement of the parties, unless otherwise required by law..

SECTION 3: By virtue of this agreement, employees do not waive any right they have
under the Federal Labor Relations Statute, unless specifically so stated herein.
Likewise, employees are not precluded by this agreement from asserting their rights in
court unless the law itself specifically imposes such a preclusion. .

any matter grievable by law. Adverse actions are excluded from the procedures of this

Union will be given an opportunity to be present:at all steps of the grievance process.
When Union representation is elected the representative. must be designated in writing
by the employee before any information or discussion is provided to the represeniative
by Management. This designation will show the name of the employee representative
who will handle the grievance for the Union. Once a representative is designated,
Management will deal only with that representative unless changed in writing.

SECTION 5:

a. Step one. When an employee desires to file a grievance he/she must, within
thirty (30) calendar days from the date of the occurrence of the event giving rise-to the
grievance, or the date the employee reasonably should have become aware of it
(whichever is later), file a written grievance with their first or second line supervisor.

b.. First line and/or second line supervisor wilt conduct a meeting with the employee,
their representative if any, and at the Union's option, an additional Union official within
fifteen (15) calendar days after receipt of the grievance. The purpose of this meeting will
be to identify, clarify, and agree on the issues involved and the resolution desired.
These items will be agreed to-and signed by both parties before the adjournment of this
meeting. The employee will present the issues and resolutions desired. The second line.
supervisor will deliver a written decision to the union representative within fifteen (15)
calendar days from the date of the meeting. Any issue or resolution not addressed at
this step will not be raised. at succeeding steps or atbitration.

c. Step two. If the resolution(s) offered at Step One do not satisfy the grievant, they
may elect to proceed to Step two. If such an election is made, the grievance with all
documentation from. Step One must be presented to the third line supervisor within
fifteen (15) calendar days following receipt of the decision from the Step One. The third
line supervisor and the Director will consider all written evidence presented within fifteen
(15) calendar days of receipt of the grievance in Step Two. The Director will render a
written decision on this matter within fifteen (15) calendar days following receipt of the
grievance at this step.

d. Step three. Final Local Review. If Step Two does not satisfy the representative,
the grieving party may elect to proceed to the final local administrative step. If such an
election is made, the grievance with all documentation from the preceding two steps
and any other evidence the grievant desires to be considered must be presented in.
writing to the DPG Commander, or Garrison. Manager within fifteen (15) calendar days
following receipt of the Step Two decision. The DPG Commander, Garrison Manager, or
their documented designee will review the information presented, investigate as.
appropriate and render a final written decision on the matter within thirty (30) calendar
days after receipt of the grievance at this step. A written decision will be presented to
the employee or representative as applicable. .

SECTION 6: If step three. of the grievance does not satisfy the employee or
representative, Management, or the Union may elect to proceed to arbitration.

SECTION 7: A grieving person or party shall have the option to:skip the first step of the
grievance procedure when it is clear that the-first step officials do not have the authority
to resolve the grievance. In any instance when Management believes a grievant has
improperly skipped a step, Management shall be limited to re-directing the grievance to
the step it finds appropriate. In no-case may a grievance otherwise timely be terminated
or denied on the basis of presentation at the wrong step.

ARTICLE 16 ARBITRATION

SECTION 1: The Union may invoke arbitration on employee or Union grievances.
Management may invoke arbitration on Management-filed grievances: Exhaustion of the
grievance machinery is.a pre-requisite to invoking arbitration unless otherwise agreed:
by the parties. If a grievance contains an issue as to unit clarification, arbitration will not
be invoked until resolution of the unit clarification:

SECTION 2: Arbitration must be invoked thirty (30) calendar days after receipt by the
grieving party of the final decision. Failure to comply with this time limit-shall constitute
acceptance of the final local review decision and the matter will not be subject to any
further review. Invocation of this provision is accomplished. by written notice of the
desire to arbitrate.

SECTION 3: At the mutual agreement of the parties, they will request a list of seven (7)
names from Federal Mediation Conciliation Service (FMCS). The. cost will be split
equally between the parties. Once the list is received the parties shall meet within fifteen
(15).calendar days. The winner of a coin flip shall strike the firstname off the list. The
parties shall alternatively strike until only one name is left on the list. The invoking party
shall notify FMCS of the election. The Arbitrator shall then contact the parties and
schedule a mutually acceptable hearing date.

152019

SECTION 4: If, for any reason, Management or Union refuses to participate in the
selection of an Arbitrator, the grieving party may unilaterally select. the name of their
desired Arbitrator from the panel and that Arbitrator shall hear the case. Either party
Shall have the power to consolidate grievances that would be ripe at the date of the
hearing for arbitration.

SECTION 5: The Arbitrator's. bill.will be borne. by both parties, unless the Arbitrator finds
that one-party is guilty of bad faith in the prosecution or defense of the grievance, in
which case the Arbitrator may impose all or part of the fees on the offending party.

SECTION 6: By mutual agreement a tape recording may be made by Management. A
copy of the tapes will be provided to the Union or the Arbitrator at their request for their
own transcription.

SECTION 7: The arbitration hearing will be held-on Management 's premises during
regular day shift hours of the Management's basic work week. The parties will notify
each other of the witnesses tobe called at least two weeks before the hearing unless
the witnesses are unknown at that time. The Arbitrator shall rule on all exhibits and
offers of evidence at the hearing. The award shall avoid the lengthy reprinting or.
paraphrasing of the parties’ briefs or positions. The aggrieved employee (if an employee
initiated grievance), Union representatives, and employees appearing as witnesses
shall be excused from duty to participate in the arbitration proceedings without loss of
pay or charge status. Employee witnesses on duty on other than the day shift will have
their tours of duty adjusted to ensure their availability for testimony:

SECTION 8: ARBITRATOR'S AUTHORITY. The Arbitrator's decision(s) shall be final
and binding and the remedy shall be effected in its entirety, except either party may file
an exception to an Arbitrator award with FLRA. The Arbitrator shall have the authority to
resolve any question of arbitration/grievance and interpret and apply the provisions of
this agreement. .

SECTION 9: The Arbitrator will determine the grievability/arbitrability before proceeding ©
to review the evidence provided on the issue. In the event the issue is not
arbitrable/grievable the Arbitrator will not spend any time reviewing the merits of the
case and will render no opinion on them. ff the dispute is arbitrable, arguments on
arbitration/grievance questions will be presented to the Arbitrator first and if feasible, a
decision rendered before presenting evidence on the issue, which is the subject of the
grievance. The purpose of this language is to keep the costs of arbitration down, if
feasible. If a decision on arbitration/grievance cannot be rendered on the same day.
without the evidence, presentation on the evidence related to the issue will continue.to
avoid use and the cost of additional arbitrator, witness, and party labor hours. In cases
where an arbitration decision has been modified or rejected by a reviewing body solely
because the remedy was ruled illegal, and the case is remanded back to the Arbitrator
for a new remedy, the parties agree the original Arbitrator will be-used to reissue the
decision.
